Learn the terms

What is BMR?

Basal Metabolic Rate is the calories your body burns at complete rest to keep you alive: breathing, circulation, and cell repair. Mifflin-St Jeor is among the most accurate equations for most adults. Katch-McArdle uses lean body mass, so it can be more accurate if you know your body fat percentage.

What is TDEE?

Total Daily Energy Expenditure is BMR multiplied by your activity level. It is the number of calories you burn in a typical day, and it is your maintenance intake.

How is the goal target set?

Roughly 7,700 kcal equals 1 kg of body fat, so a daily gap of 500 kcal is about 0.45 kg (1 lb) per week. You can adjust the gap with the slider. Maintain keeps you at TDEE. Crash diets below 1,200 kcal (women) or 1,500 kcal (men) are not recommended without medical supervision, so the calculator will not go below those levels.

How accurate is the timeline?

It assumes your activity and intake stay steady. Real progress varies with water weight, sleep, and stress, and weight loss usually slows as you get lighter. Use it as a rough plan and review your progress every 2 to 3 weeks.

What is BMI?

Body Mass Index is weight divided by height squared. Under 18.5 is underweight, 18.5 to 24.9 is healthy range, 25 to 29.9 is overweight, and 30 or more is obese. BMI does not measure body fat directly, so treat it as a rough guide.

Why are the macros split this way?

Protein supports muscle and fullness (4 kcal per gram), carbohydrates fuel activity (4 kcal per gram), and fat supports hormones and vitamin absorption (9 kcal per gram). The split shown is a common general starting point.

Limits

These are estimates, not medical advice. Weigh yourself for 2 to 3 weeks and adjust. See a doctor or dietitian first if you are pregnant, have a medical condition, take medication that affects weight, have a history of an eating disorder, or are under 18.
